-- 3 of 29 --

pilot
concept &
rationale
Candidex proposes a targeted pilot
employment pathway designed to help
MSD clients transition into sustainable
roles as unqualified early childhood
teachers (unqualified Kaiako) within
licensed early learning centres. The
initiative blends work-readiness
coaching, sector-specific training, and
brokered employment placements
through Candidexʼs established
nationwide network of early childhood
employers.
The pilot would launch in Wellington as
an initial testing ground, focusing on a
first intake of approx 45 MSD
candidates, split across the 9 regional
Wellington offices, who are either work-
ready or nearing readiness for
employment. With short, focused
support, these participants will be
equipped to step into Early Childhood
Education (ECE) roles where they can
develop valuable, transferable skills
while contributing meaningfully to the
sector. The long-term goal is to create a
replicable and scalable model that MSD
can roll out nationally across the broader
education and social services workforce.
We propose utilising the MSD office
locations to deliver the in-class portions
of the programme. This provides a
familar and consistent location for MSD
candidates.
The proposed pathway is grounded in
MSDʼs evidence-based understanding
that employment assistance is most
effective when it combines skills
development, employer engagement,
and structured qualification pathways.
By embedding these proven elements,
the pilot offers a comprehensive and
sustainable model that supports MSDʼs
objectives of increasing employment
participation in social and community
services, while building capability in the
early learning sector.
This concept mirrors key design features
of successful MSD programmes, such
as youth and training pathways, by
integrating work-based learning,
targeted pastoral support, and clear
progression routes into further study or
higher-skilled employment. It represents
a practical, collaborative, and scalable
solution to workforce challenges while
delivering meaningful social and
economic value.

Target

The primary target group for this pilot
comprises MSD candidates who:
Are currently receiving income
support and are either unemployed
or under-employed.
Have an interest in working with
children and can meet the Education

Passing a full Childrenʼs Worker

Compliance completed as

the Candidex team.
Having no specified offences
that would prevent employment
in a core childrenʼs worker role,

worker exemption where

Being able to meet early learning
service child protection and
safety policies, including ongoing

years and complying with
supervision, conduct, and

expectations in an ECE

This group represents individuals who
are motivated to enter the workforce and

next steps &
purpose
Candidex is seeking MSD investment to
co-design and pilot this pathway over an
initial two-quarter period, supporting 3
cohorts of 15 through an intensive 12-
week programme.
The proposed structure includes six
weeks of in-person delivery, followed by
six weeks of ongoing job search and
employment support, with the precise
balance and mode of delivery open to
negotiation with MSD.
Across the pilot, a total of 45 MSD
candidates would be supported, with
jointly agreed KPIs and evaluation
settings used to track outcomes such as
placements, retention, and progression.
Taking into account the current Skills fo
Industry KPI metrics this would give us
an attendance target of 40 and a
placement target of 28 candidates into
permanent employment - with 20
remaining off the benefit for a minimum
of 182 days. These are our minimum
targets.
If the pilot proves successful, the model
can be refined and expanded into other
regions and adjacent roles across the
broader education and social and
community services workforce.
The target commencement date for the
pilot is 1 March 2026 (or prior), subject
to MSD approval and finalisation of
design parameters.
The programme is designed to equip
unqualified candidates with the core
knowledge, competencies, and practical
experience needed to step confidently
into entry-level roles in Early Childhood
Education and teacher aide positions. It
focuses on building a strong foundation
in safe practice, professional conduct,
and effective engagement with tamariki
and whānau, aligned with expectations
of New Zealand ECE services and
school-based learning support roles.
By combining sector-relevant content,
work-readiness coaching, and real-
world exposure to ECE and school
environments, the programme aims to
create a practical, employment-focused
pathway that both meets employer
needs and advances MSD candidates
toward sustainable work and future
training opportunities.

New Zealandʼs early childhood
education (ECE) market is nationally
significant, with 4,483 licensed services
operated by 1,941 individual providers,
representing an estimated $2.1 billion
sector.
Auckland is the largest region,
accounting for around one-third of all
ECE enrolments (approximately 65,000
enrolments, or 33.7%), followed by
Canterbury (about 24,700 enrolments,
13.1%) and Waikato (around 20,500
enrolments, 11.0%). Other key regions
include Wellington (circa 18,300
enrolments, 9.9%), Bay of Plenty
(around 14,800 enrolments, 8.0%), and
Manawatū‑Whanganui (about 9,500
enrolments, 5.1%), with the remaining
enrolments spread across Hawkeʼs Bay,
Otago, Northland, Southland, and a wide
range of rural communities.
Across these regions, nearly half of all
services operate waiting lists, with
pressure most acute in high-demand
urban centres and geographically
isolated rural areas. This uneven
regional distribution, combined with
persistent workforce shortages,
underpins the need for targeted
recruitment solutions that can respond
to both metropolitan and regional labour
market conditions. This is where
Candidex comes in.....

ECE workforce
New Zealandʼs ECE workforce modelling
indicates that unqualified and
below‑degree educators will remain an
essential part of the workforce for at
least the next 3–5 years. The ECE
workforce report shows that a large
share of the current workforce are
unqualified or working towards level 2–3
qualifications, with the Level 4 NZ
Certificate in ECE and Care identified as
a dominant non‑degree qualification,
meaning thousands of educators are
working in centres without a degree but
are central to daily care and education.
These roles are used as core staffing,
not just “nice to haveˮ extras.They also
act as a pipeline into future degree‑level
teacher roles.
National modelling suggests the overall
supply of qualified ECE teachers is
roughly sufficient in aggregate, but it
also highlights regional and service‑type
shortages and ongoing retention
problems, so services are forced to staff
above minimum ratios using a mix of
qualified teachers, ECE support workers
and in‑training staff to stay open. Sector
surveys in 2024–25 report that around
one‑third of ECE teachers are
considering leaving the sector, which
reinforces employersʼ need for a steady
inflow of new teachers in support roles
just to maintain existing capacity.
Teacher aids
For teacher aides, Budget 2025 commits
to the “largest boost to learning support
in a generation,ˮ funding an extra 2
million teacher‑aide hours per year by
2029, ramping from 225,000 additional
hours in 2026, 450,000 in 2027,
675,000 in 2028, and 900,000 extra
hours per year from 2029 onwards. A
substantial proportion of these hours are
targeted to early intervention and Years
1–8, which includes new‑entrant classes,
and are delivered by paraprofessional
staff rather than qualified teachers.
Early‑years and new‑entrant classes
are a key focus for the extra
hours.These roles are
teacher‑aide/paraprofessional
positions, not additional qualified
teacher FTE. Unions have called for
“a teacher aide in every classroomˮ,
arguing current support is still well
below need.
Taken together, this data shows strong
and growing employer demand for
unqualified and in‑training educators in
ECE (support workers/kaiāwhina) and
for teacher aides in early learning and
new‑entrant classes, both as core staff
and as a structured pipeline into future
qualified teacher roles

pathways &
progression
Once in employment, participants will be encouraged and supported to
continue their learning through established ECE and education‑support
pathways.
Our intention is to invite representatives from the various education providers
to discuss these options in person, providing candidates a chance to ask
questions and to decide what the best plan for progression could be for them.
In practice, this means they can start or continue NZQA‑approved certificates
at Levels 2–4, progress into Diplomas at Levels 5–6, and, over time, move into
initial teacher‑education programmes at Level 7 that lead to teacher
registration. These are designed to be completed alongside work, so
participants can build qualifications while contributing as ECE support workers
or teacher aides.
ECE support workers can enrol in the New Zealand Certificate in Early
Childhood Education and Care (Levels 3–4), which is explicitly recognised
as a pathway into the Level 5–6 ECE diplomas.
With experience plus Level 3–4, they can progress to the New Zealand
Diploma in Early Childhood Education and Care (Level 5–6), which many
providers credit directly into a Bachelor of Teaching (ECE).
Teacher aides can complete targeted Education Support / Teacher Aide
programmes at Levels 3–4, and then staircase into ECE or primary teaching
qualifications if they want to train as fully qualified teachers.
Adults aged 20+ can often use work experience plus these lower‑level

Our Tuakana Teina pilot programme in Porirua has been a success, focusing
on a holistic approach to work readiness and job placement for candidates
aged 18-24 years old.
Scope:
The pilot involved an intensive 10-week cycle, with a focus on CV, navigating
job boards and recruitment agencies, interview skills, workplace expectations,
and physical wellbeing. Participants received individualised coaching and
practical support.
Launched on 25th March 2025 and concluding 20th June 2025.
MSD supplied an initial list of 65 candidates who had been referred by their
case manager. All were emailed the application and pre-screen form by
their case manager.
12 candidates responded directly.
Candidex phoned all 65 candidates on the list to pre-screen for
commitment and to follow up on the initial email referral to the pilot
programme. Many had not seen/received the initial email. Highlighting the
importance of the direct outreach by our team.
A large number pre-selected themselves out of the programme for various
reasons such as motivation, mental health challenges, and some simply
did not respond to the outreach. Participation was not compulsory.
We ended with a total of 32 clients fully screened and interested in
participating in the programme. As the agreed pilot volume was 10 we held
the remaining client profiles for either future cohorts or to bring in to the
programme should a participant drop out.
